# AnnSarthi Environmental & Social Impact Methodology
**Scientific Accounting Framework for Food Waste Redistribution (SIH26234)**

---

## 1. Overview & Regulatory Grounding

AnnSarthi quantifies the environmental and social returns of surplus food rescue using standardized Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) protocols established by the **United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization (FAO)**, the **United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP)**, and the **World Resources Institute (WRI)**.

> **Transparency Declaration**:
> In accordance with our Non-Negotiable Honesty Principle, AnnSarthi explicitly declares all environmental impact values as **scientific estimates** derived from peer-reviewed LCA multipliers rather than real-time sensor measurements.

---

## 2. Quantitative Environmental Equations

### 2.1 Greenhouse Gas Emissions Mitigation ($\text{CO}_2\text{e}$)
When surplus food is discarded into unmanaged landfills, anaerobic decomposition generates methane ($\text{CH}_4$), a potent greenhouse gas with a Global Warming Potential (GWP) 28 times greater than carbon dioxide over a 100-year horizon. Furthermore, diverting edible food avoids the redundant agricultural and transportation emissions required to replace those calories.

$$\text{CO}_2\text{e Prevented (kg)} = M_{\text{food}} \times 2.5$$

Where:
- $M_{\text{food}}$ is the verified mass of edible surplus food redistributed (in kilograms).
- **$2.5\text{ kg CO}_2\text{e / kg}$** is the composite emission factor derived from the *FAO Food Wastage Footprint (FWF)* for cooked mixed meals in developing Asian economies:
  - $\sim 1.9\text{ kg CO}_2\text{e}$ from avoided landfill methane generation.
  - $\sim 0.6\text{ kg CO}_2\text{e}$ from upstream lifecycle embedded production.

---

### 2.2 Embedded Virtual Water Conservation
Agriculture consumes approximately 70% of global freshwater withdrawals. Throwing away food squanders the virtual water embedded in irrigation, grain cultivation, and food preparation.

$$\text{Water Conserved (Liters)} = M_{\text{food}} \times 850$$

Where:
- **$850\text{ Liters / kg}$** represents the weighted average virtual water footprint for Indian staple meal preparations (incorporating wheat rotis, rice, lentils, seasonal vegetables, and dairy curds) based on Water Footprint Network benchmarks.

---

### 2.3 Municipal Landfill Space Diversion
Food waste forms the largest single component of municipal solid waste in Indian Tier-1 and Tier-2 cities, straining municipal landfills such as Devguradiya in Indore.

$$\text{Landfill Volume Saved (m}^3\text{)} = M_{\text{food}} \times 0.0018$$

Where:
- **$0.0018\text{ m}^3\text{ / kg}$** is based on the compacted in-situ density of organic municipal waste ($\approx 550\text{ kg/m}^3$).

## 3. Social Nourishment Metrics

$$\text{Meals Equivalent Provided} = \frac{M_{\text{food}}}{0.42\text{ kg / meal}}$$

- Standard Indian nutritional guidelines assume a nutritious lunch/dinner serving weighs approximately $400\text{ to }450\text{ grams}$ per adult beneficiary.
